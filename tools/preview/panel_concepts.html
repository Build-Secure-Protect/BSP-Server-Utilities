Display panels: three block looks, and the settings screen: three looks

Set B of the mock-ups. One Display Panel block (D14) that joins with its neighbours up to 8 wide and 6 high on a wall (D15), placed, set up and broken by admins only (D16). Right-clicking it opens its settings: the mode (Text, Image, Staff online, Action log) and, for Text, the line editor (D17); for Image, the source, the fit and the refresh (D18 to D21). The staff and log faces are set C.

B1. The block (three.js; a single block on the left, a 4 x 2 wall on the right)

Look A: slim bezel

A thin dark bezel round the outside of the whole panel and nothing between the blocks, so a joined wall reads as one screen; a matte black face; a small teal light in the bottom-right corner of the bezel. The text glows (full bright) so it reads at night. Closest to BSP-Core's Score Screen.

Look B: holo glass

A dark glass face over a back plate, with a glowing teal edge round the outside and faint scan lines across the face; a pale metal rim. The most "screen"-like; the glass lets a little of the wall colour through at the edges.

Look C: framed notice board

A thicker iron-grey frame with rivets at the corners and the face sunk a pixel behind it, like a notice board; when blocks join, the inner frame pieces go and only the outer frame stays. Reads as a sign more than a screen.

B2. The panel's settings (violet, admins only)

Show:

Look A: mode chips, a preview and the line list

The modes as chips along the top. Left: a preview of this panel's face in its real proportions (here 4 x 2) as players will see it. Right: one row per line with its text box, then the size (S M L XL), colour, alignment and bold; the picked line's colour opens the 16 Minecraft colours plus a #RRGGBB box. + ADD LINE, the background colour and SAVE at the bottom.

Decisions for set B (defaults first)

  1. B1. The block's look. Default: look A, the slim bezel round the whole panel with nothing between blocks. Or look B, the holo glass with a glowing edge. Or look C, the framed notice board with rivets.
  2. B2. Light. Default: what the panel shows is drawn at full brightness, so it reads the same at night and in caves. Or it is lit by the world's light like a sign.
  3. B3. The settings screen's look. Default: look A, mode chips, a preview on the left and one row per line with its own size, colour, alignment and bold. Or look B, a big preview you click lines on, with an inspector under it. Or look C, one text box with a toolbar.
  4. B4. Text sizes. Default: four sizes (S, M, L, XL) that scale with the panel, so a line takes the same share of the panel's height on a 2-high or a 6-high panel. Or sizes fixed per block, so a bigger panel holds more lines of the same size.
  5. B5. Colours. Default: the 16 Minecraft chat colours as swatches plus a #RRGGBB box, and & codes inside a line for a word in another colour. Or the 16 swatches only.
  6. B6. How much text. Default: up to 16 lines of up to 80 characters each. Or up to 8 lines.
  7. B7. Background. Default: a colour from a few dark swatches, or clear so the wall behind shows. Or always the panel's black.
  8. B8. Saving. Default: changes show on the preview at once and reach the panel in the world when you press SAVE (CLOSE keeps the panel as it was). Or every change reaches the world as you type.
  9. B9. Image mode. Default: as drawn, a choice of web address or a file from the server folder, the three fits, REFRESH NOW and a refresh timer (off, 5, 15 or 60 minutes), with a line saying what was loaded. Or the web address only.
