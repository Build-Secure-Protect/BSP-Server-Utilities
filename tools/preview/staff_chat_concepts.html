Staff panel: mock-up set Q, chat

Three looks for the Chat tab on a phone (D261 to D263): public chat from every server, live, the server on each line, kept 30 days; speaking as yourself with [Staff] or as [Server], to one server or the network (D262). On a computer the chat stays the 360-pixel column on the right (D292); the look picked here also sets how lines are drawn in that column. Below them, three looks for a line sent from the panel as players see it in game. Made-up names and lines.

A. A plain stream, like the computer's column

One line per message: time, the server with its colour, the name with any tags (D267), the text. Server chips along the top filter the stream. At the foot two choosers, AS and TO, then the box and SEND. Tapping a name opens that player's sheet.

A1, the stream (Vexa's tag shows beside her name)
A2, the AS / TO chooser open (a Moderator: [Server] dimmed)

B. The game's own chat

Lines drawn as the game draws them, on a game-like background, with each server's small image in front; the densest look, most lines on screen. The box at the foot looks like the game's chat box, and while typing a strip shows exactly what players will see before SEND.

B1, the chat (the server tabs filter it)
B2, typing: "Players will see" above the box

C. A conversation, with heads

Each player's lines grouped under their head, name, server and time, as in a messaging app; your own lines on the right in violet, [Server] lines in the middle in gold. The round key at the foot is the voice you speak with (your head ringed violet, or the server's mark). A long press on a line opens a menu: the player's sheet, a note about them, copy, only that server.

C1, the conversation (scrolled up: "3 new lines" to jump down)
C2, a long press on one of Vexa's lines

In game: a line sent from the panel

What players see when Ana_Mod speaks from the panel, among ordinary chat, then a [Server] line. In every look, hovering the line shows who and where it came from. G3's mark is a small picture from this mod's font, like the server images, so it needs the new client that the notes count (D266) needs anyway; an old client would show the plain words instead.

G1, a tag then a colon: "[Staff] Ana_Mod: …", "[Server] …"
G2, inside the usual brackets: "<[Staff] Ana_Mod> …", the same shape as players' lines
G3, a small staff mark and "»", the line in soft violet, the hover drawn

Choices

  1. Q1. The Chat tab's look. A: a plain stream, the same as the computer's column, one line per message with the server's colour. B: the game's own chat on a game background, the most lines on screen, with a strip showing what players will see. C: a conversation with heads and grouped lines, your own on the right, a long press for the player's sheet or a note.
  2. Q2. A panel line in game. G1: "[Staff] Ana_Mod: text" with [Staff] in violet, and "[Server] text" with [Server] in gold. G2: "<[Staff] Ana_Mod> text", inside the brackets players' own lines use. G3: a small staff mark, then "Ana_Mod » text" in soft violet, and a server mark with gold text for [Server] (needs the new client).
  3. Q3. Sending. SEND puts the line out at once, and every line goes into the Action Log with your name. Or SEND asks SURE? only for [Server] lines to the whole network. Or SEND always asks SURE?.
  4. Q4. Who may speak as [Server]. Admins and Owners only, as with Broadcast (D250); Moderators see it dimmed with "Admin" (D278), and every rank speaks as themselves. Or every rank may speak as [Server].
  5. Q5. The Chat tab's badge. A red number for lines that name you or ask for staff ("staff", "admin", "mod", "help"), and a plain dot for any other new line. Or a number for every line since you last looked (99+). Or no badge at all.
  6. Q6. Older lines. Scrolling up loads older lines, back to 30 days, and a search key finds lines by words, player, server or day. Or scrolling up only, with no search.
  7. Q7. What the hover says in game. "Ana_Mod, staff, from the staff panel" on a [Staff] line, and "From the staff" on a [Server] line (who sent it stays in the Action Log, as with announcements, D241). Or the sender's name on [Server] lines too.
