Staff panel: Worlds, regions and drawing on the map: concepts

Four sets, three looks each, for the owner's brief of 2026-11-26. BC is the new WORLDS tab: the servers, a server's dimensions, and the Region Guard regions in each. BD is the region editor. BE is the Map tab's new Regions window and the coloured rectangles. BF is drawing a new region from two points on the map. Pick one look per set, or mix them. The terrain is a drawn stand-in for BlueMap's; names, places and numbers are made up.

BC Worlds tab · BD Region editor · BE Regions window on the map · BF Drawing a region

BC. The WORLDS tab

Each look shows the grid of servers, then what you see after clicking Frostwarf. WORLDS sits in the menu between Map and Totems.

BC A. Tiles, then dimension cards and a table

Four tiles across: the server's name, online or not, players, tick time, its dimensions, and its regions counted by kind in their colours
Frostwarf: one card per dimension with its counts; the chosen dimension's regions in a table, sortable, with MAP › and EDIT › on each row

BC B. Map tiles, then three columns

Three tiles across, each with a small picture of its Overworld map and its regions outlined
Frostwarf: dimensions on the left, the regions grouped by kind in the middle, the chosen region's summary on the right with MAP › and EDIT ›

BC C. Compact tiles, then a tree

Five narrow tiles across: name, players and counts only; offline servers greyed with when they were last heard from
Frostwarf: every dimension as a heading that folds, the regions inside as a tree by parent (a mine's shell under its mine, plots under the town), the global region first

BD. The region editor

Opened from EDIT › on WORLDS or on the map. Each look shows a different kind of region so all the parts are seen: A a protected region, B a player's claim (its Visitors, Owners and Members lists), C a mine (its block mix). Every look has the same parts; only the layout differs. Changes wait in gold until SAVE.

BD A. A drawer over the list

A drawer slides in from the right over WORLDS: general settings, bounds, people, then every flag by group as Allow / Deny / Inherit, showing what Inherit gives and where from

BD B. A full page with sections

The whole page: sections down the left with a gold count of unsaved changes; the flags as a table with one column per audience for claims and plots

BD C. A card with tabs and flag tiles

A card with tabs (Settings, Flags, People, Mine, Triggers); the mine's block mix as a bar and rows; flags shown as tiles that cycle Allow, Deny, Inherit on a click

BE. The Regions window on the Map tab

A second floating window beside the players window, with the same look: see-through, dragged by its strip, sized from its corner, folded with "–". Whichever was touched last is on top. The rectangles are BlueMap markers the page adds itself; a click on a row or a rectangle flies the map to the region and lights it.

BE A. Grouped by kind, with colour chips

Kind chips switch each colour's rectangles on and off; the chosen region is outlined in its colour with a gold label, and a small card shows its main facts with EDIT ›

BE B. A tree, with an eye on every row

Regions as a tree by parent, an eye on each row to show or hide its rectangle; every rectangle carries its name on the map

BE C. Kind tabs and "in view only"

Tabs by kind, a switch to list only the regions in view, and the other regions faded while one is chosen; the players window folded to its strip

BF. Drawing a new region from two points

From NEW REGION in the Regions window (Admins and Owners). BlueMap's page reports the block under each click, so the two corners come from the map itself. The region is made on the server that owns the world shown, by Region Guard, with Region Guard's own checks.

BF A. Two clicks, then a form

A gold banner says which corner to click; after the second the outline is drawn dashed between the pins and a form asks for the id, kind, priority and heights

BF B. Two clicks, then handles to adjust

After the two clicks the outline gets handles on its corners and edges with its size shown; the Regions window turns into the form, and Region Guard's check runs as it changes

BF C. Typed corners with a pick key

A form with both corners as numbers; PICK beside each puts a crosshair on the map that follows the pointer with its block, and a click fills that corner; SNAP TO CHUNKS rounds the corners out

What every look shares

  1. Colours by kind. Region red, mine gold, claim green, plot for sale light green with a dashed edge, open area for buying blue, the global region grey (never drawn as a rectangle: it is the whole world). Changeable in the decisions.
  2. Where the regions come from. Each game server answers for its own worlds, through Region Guard, when the tab or window opens and again when a region changes. A server that is off shows its tile greyed and no regions.
  3. Who may change things. Every rank sees; Admins and Owners edit, create and remove (Moderators see the editor read-only with "Admin" on its keys), as for items and bans.
  4. Safety. An edit carries the region's version: if someone changed it in game meanwhile, SAVE is refused and the new values are shown. Removing a region asks "Sure? Remove" for three seconds. Every save, creation and removal goes in the panel's log and the game's action log.
